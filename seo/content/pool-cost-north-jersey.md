## Why do North Jersey pool prices differ from national averages?

Four things push North Jersey above the national figures that AI assistants and cost calculators usually quote:

1. **Labor and insurance costs.** [[GGP FILL: one or two sentences on local crew and insurance costs, without naming figures unless GGP is willing to publish them.]]
2. **Permits and engineering.** Most Bergen and Passaic towns require a sealed survey and, increasingly, a grading and drainage plan. Soil-moving and impervious-coverage ordinances add engineering fees that a Sun Belt builder never sees. See the permit table below.
3. **Rock.** The Watchung ridges (basalt) run through Essex, Passaic, Union, Somerset and Morris, and the New Jersey Highlands (Precambrian gneiss) cover western Passaic, Morris, Sussex and Warren. Shallow bedrock turns a one-day dig into a hammer-and-haul job. The NJ Geological Survey's statewide bedrock map shows where these units sit (https://www.nj.gov/dep/njgs/enviroed/freedwn/Bedrock2017.pdf, 2017).
4. **Water.** Low-lying parts of Bergen and Hudson sit on former Glacial Lake Hackensack sediments and the Hackensack Meadowlands, where groundwater is close to the surface. That means dewatering during the dig and hydrostatic relief valves in the shell.

## How long do pool permits take in North Jersey towns?

New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code gives a construction official **20 business days** to grant or deny a complete permit application; if nothing happens within that window the application is treated as denied for appeal purposes (N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.16, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-5-23-2-16). In practice the clock only starts after **zoning approval**, which is where most of the real waiting happens, especially in towns that want a grading or soil-moving plan. ## How is the North Jersey Pool Cost Index calculated?

**Source data.** Every GGP figure is drawn from executed contracts and approved change orders for inground pools built in New Jersey by Good Guys Pools between [[GGP FILL: start date]] and [[GGP FILL: end date]]. Estimates that did not become contracts are excluded. Repair and renovation jobs are excluded from the build tables and will get their own index. [[GGP FILL: confirm]]

**What counts as a "standard build."** Shell, standard single-speed or variable-speed pump and filter [[GGP FILL: which]], one light, basic coping, and the minimum patio required to pass final inspection. Anything else is reported in the add-on table.

**Statistics.** We report the **median** rather than the mean so one very large project does not distort a county. Ranges are the lowest and highest contract price in the cell. Cells with fewer than [[GGP FILL: minimum n]] projects are suppressed or grouped to protect customer privacy. Sample size (n) is shown with each figure.

**Geography.** Projects are assigned to the county of the job address, not the billing address.

**Permit timelines.** Measured from the date the complete application was stamped received to the date the permit was issued, taken from the permit documents themselves. Zoning and construction are tracked separately because the UCC 20-business-day clock only applies to the construction permit.

**Industry references.** Where a national figure is shown, the source, URL and year are stated and the figure is labeled "industry reference, not GGP data." We do not blend outside numbers into GGP medians.

**Update cadence.** Every January, with the previous year's projects added and the changelog below updated. Figures are not adjusted for inflation between updates.

**Limitations.** This is one builder's data, so it reflects the projects GGP wins, not the whole North Jersey market. Use it as a grounded starting point, then get site-specific quotes.

## FAQ

**How much does a gunite pool cost in Bergen County, NJ?**
[[GGP FILL: Bergen gunite median and range from the table above, with n.]] That is the installed contract price for a standard build; spa, heater, auto cover and extended patio are added on top (see the add-on table).

**Is a vinyl liner pool really the cheapest option in New Jersey?**
Usually the lowest entry price, yes. [[GGP FILL: GGP vinyl vs gunite median difference.]] Budget for a liner replacement every [[GGP FILL: years GGP typically sees]] when comparing lifetime cost. See our gunite vs vinyl vs fiberglass comparison for the trade-offs.

**Why did my quote include rock excavation?**
Because your lot likely sits on the Watchung basalt ridges or the Highlands gneiss. Builders cannot see bedrock depth from the surface; a test pit or a neighbor's experience is the best early signal. [[GGP FILL: how GGP handles rock, e.g. test pit before contract, rock clause per cubic yard.]]

**Do pool prices in NJ include the permit fees?**
[[GGP FILL: whether GGP's contract price includes permit fees, survey and engineering, or lists them separately.]] Municipal permit fees vary by town and are set by local ordinance under the UCC fee schedule.

**Does a pool need a fence in New Jersey?**
Yes. New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code requires a barrier at least 48 inches high around residential pools, with self-closing, self-latching gates, following IRC Appendix G / the International Swimming Pool and Spa Code depending on the permit date (N.J.A.C. 5:23; see the NJ DCA Construction Code Communicator, https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/publications/pdf_ccc/CCC_Smr_2020.pdf). Some towns require taller fences; confirm with your construction office.
