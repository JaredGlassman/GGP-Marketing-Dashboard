# Inground Pool Permits in Bergen County, NJ: What to Expect

**Short answer:** Yes. Every inground pool in New Jersey needs permits. In Bergen County that usually means (1) **zoning approval** from your town for setbacks and lot coverage, (2) a **building permit** and an **electrical permit** under the NJ Uniform Construction Code, and (3) passed **inspections**, including the pool barrier (fence), before you can swim. Expect roughly [[GGP FILL: typical weeks from GGP permit history]] from application to approval, depending on the town.

## Who issues pool permits in Bergen County?
Permits come from your **municipality's construction office**, not from the county. Each of Bergen's 70 towns runs its own zoning and construction departments, which is why rules and timing vary between, say, Paramus and Saddle River.

## The approvals, in order
1. **Survey and site plan.** A current property survey showing the pool, patio, equipment pad, and distances to property lines.
2. **Zoning review.** The town checks setbacks (distance from rear and side lot lines), **lot coverage / impervious coverage**, and any easements. Many Bergen towns also want a **grading and drainage (stormwater) plan** for pools.
3. **Building permit.** Covers the pool structure under the NJ Uniform Construction Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23).
4. **Electrical permit.** Bonding, grounding, pump and light circuits, and GFCI protection.
5. **Plumbing / gas permit,** if you are adding a gas heater.
6. **Call 811 (NJ One Call)** before excavation. State law requires it.
7. **Inspections** during and after construction: bonding, steel (gunite), electrical, final, and the **barrier**.

## Pool fence (barrier) rules in New Jersey
New Jersey's code requires a barrier around residential pools. The common requirements are a **minimum 48-inch height**, self-closing and self-latching gates, and limits on gaps and climbable footholds. Some towns add their own rules. **Confirm with your construction office.** We design the fence into the plan up front so the final inspection isn't held up.

## Common mistakes that delay pool permits
- An old survey that doesn't show a recent addition or shed
- Equipment pad placed inside a side setback
- Forgetting that patio/decking counts toward impervious coverage
- Fence plans that don't show gate hardware

## FAQ
**Do I need a permit for a pool repair or resurfacing in NJ?**
Usually not for cosmetic resurfacing. Electrical, structural, or gas work generally does need one. Ask your construction office. [[GGP FILL: what GGP sees in practice]]

**Can I start digging before the permit is issued?**
No. Work started before a permit can be stopped and fined, and you may have to undo it.
