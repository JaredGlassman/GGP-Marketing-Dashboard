# Pool Repair, Renovation or Rebuild? How to Tell

**Short answer:** If the problem is limited to one component (a pump, a leaking fitting, a small patch of surface), it's a **repair**. If the surface, tile and coping are worn throughout but the shell is sound, it's a **renovation**. If the shell itself is moving, cracking structurally, or losing water through the structure, you are looking at a **rebuild**. New Jersey's freeze-thaw winters speed up all three.

## Signs you need a repair
- Water loss of more than about 1/4 inch per day beyond evaporation (do the bucket test)
- Pump that won't prime, a tripping breaker, or a heater fault
- A single loose tile or a small surface chip

## Signs you need a renovation
- Rough, stained or etched plaster across the whole pool
- Cracked or heaving coping, or many loose waterline tiles
- A faded, wrinkled or brittle vinyl liner
- Outdated equipment you'd like to replace anyway (variable-speed pump, automation, LED lights)

## Signs it may be a rebuild
- Structural cracks that reopen after patching
- A shell that has shifted or settled (often from poor original backfill or groundwater)
- Repeated leaks in the structure, not the plumbing

## North Jersey factors
- **Freeze-thaw:** water trapped behind tile and under coping expands. Proper winterizing prevents a lot of renovation work.
- **High water tables** in parts of Bergen and Passaic: never drain a pool without checking groundwater first, because the shell can float.
- **Older pools:** many 1970s–90s pools in Bergen County have original plumbing and no modern bonding.
