# Best Time to Build an Inground Pool in New Jersey: A Month-by-Month Timeline

The best time to start an inground pool in New Jersey is fall or winter. Design, surveys and permits take months, and spring is the busiest season for permit offices and crews. Sign in October through January, dig when the ground thaws in March or April, and you can be swimming by summer. Homeowners who book in winter get first pick of spring dig dates.

## Why should I start planning a New Jersey pool in the fall?

Most homeowners start thinking about a pool in May, when it is hot and the neighbors are swimming. By then the builders worth hiring in Bergen, Passaic and Morris are already digging the jobs they signed in winter, and the permit office has a stack of spring applications in front of yours.

Starting in fall flips that. Between September and January:

- **Design happens without pressure.** Site visits, layout, equipment selection and the patio plan take several meetings. There is no rush when the dig is months away.
- **The survey and engineering get done.** Most North Jersey towns want a current sealed survey, and many Bergen towns want a grading and drainage plan. Surveyors and engineers are also slower in spring.
- **Permits are filed into a quieter queue.** Zoning review is where most of the waiting happens, and a November application is reviewed by an office that is not fielding spring deck, fence and pool applications at the same time.
- **Your dig slot is reserved.** Builders schedule spring excavation in order of signed contracts and approved permits. [[GGP FILL: how GGP sequences its spring schedule, e.g. "permit in hand by March 1 gets an April dig slot."]]
- **Long-lead items are ordered.** Auto covers, custom tile and some equipment have lead times that stretch in spring. Fiberglass shells have factory lead times as well.

## How long do pool permits take in New Jersey, and why do they drive the schedule?

New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code requires the construction official to grant or deny a complete permit application within **20 business days**; if nothing is issued in that window the application is treated as denied so you can appeal (N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.16, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-5-23-2-16). That sounds fast, and the building-permit part often is. The schedule risk is everything that happens **before** the construction clock starts:

1. **Zoning review.** Setbacks, lot coverage, impervious coverage, equipment location, easements. Some towns review in a week; some take a month or more, and a coverage problem means a variance hearing that can add months.
2. **Grading, drainage or soil-moving approval.** Common in Bergen towns; may require an engineer's plan and a separate review by the town engineer.
3. **Shade tree, wetlands or flood hazard review.** Lots near the Passaic, Saddle, Hackensack or Ramapo rivers may need NJDEP flood hazard approval or a permit-by-rule under N.J.A.C. 7:13. [[VERIFY current provisions.]]
4. **Resubmissions.** An incomplete survey or a missing fence detail restarts the review.

## Can you build a pool in the winter in New Jersey?

Partly. Design, engineering and permitting are winter work and should be. Physical construction is weather-dependent:

- **Excavation** is possible in a mild winter but frozen ground, mud and short days slow it down. [[GGP FILL: whether GGP digs in Dec-Feb and under what conditions.]]
- **Gunite** should not be shot when temperatures will drop below freezing during the cure, so the shell usually waits for late March or April. [[VERIFY: GGP's temperature threshold and curing practice.]]
- **Vinyl liners** are installed warm so the material stretches; cold-weather liner installs are avoided.
- **Masonry and plaster** are also temperature-sensitive.

So the realistic winter plan is: everything on paper done by February, shovel in the ground as soon as the frost is out.

**Can a pool be dug when the ground is frozen?**
Frozen topsoil can be broken, but frost, mud and short days make winter excavation slow and messy. Most North Jersey digs wait for late March or April. [[GGP FILL: GGP's practice.]]

**Do I need the fence before I can fill the pool?**
The barrier must pass inspection before the pool is used. New Jersey's code requires a minimum 48-inch barrier with self-closing, self-latching gates (IRC Appendix G / ISPSC as adopted under N.J.A.C. 5:23; see https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/publications/pdf_ccc/CCC_Smr_2020.pdf). Some towns require more. Design the fence in October, not June.

**When is the best time to renovate or resurface an existing pool in NJ?**
Late summer through fall, after the swim season, so the work cures over winter and the pool opens on time in spring. [[GGP FILL: confirm GGP's renovation season.]]
